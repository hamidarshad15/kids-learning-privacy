Privacy Policy: Kids Learning: ABC & More

Effective date: 27 October 2026

Last updated: 29 October 2026 (developer name only; no change to how information is handled)

This policy explains what information the Android app Kids Learning: ABC & More ("the app") uses, where it goes, and the choices parents have. The app is made by Muhammad Hamid Arshad ("we", "us").

If you have a question about privacy, or want us to delete a suggestion you sent, email hamid.arshad15@gmail.com.

In short

  • The app is made for young children. There is no account and no login, and the app does not ask children for their name or any other personal details.
  • Learning progress (stars, stickers, pictures) is kept on your phone.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob, set up for children: not personalised and limited to general-audience (rated G) ads. Google still receives some technical information to show ads, as described below.
  • Parents can optionally send us a suggestion. It goes to our Google Firebase database.
  • Payments ("Remove ads" and optional tips) are handled only by Google Play. We never see payment details.
  • We do not sell any information.

1. Information that stays on your phone

The app saves these on your phone so it works the next time it is opened:

  • quiz stars and the stickers your child has collected
  • the pictures your child makes in "My World", and its settings (for example day or night)
  • whether "Remove ads" has been bought on this phone
  • suggestions written by a parent that have not been sent yet (see section 2)

This information is not sent to us. Pictures from "My World" are saved to the phone's photo gallery only when your child taps the save button.

You can reset stars and stickers in the app's Parents area. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, removes all of this information from the phone.

Device backup. The app uses Android's standard backup setting. If backup is turned on for the phone, Android may copy the app's stored data — including the progress, settings and any unsent suggestion described above, and technical data stored by Google's components — to a private area of your Google account, and may also copy it when you move to a new phone. These backups are created and managed by Android and Google; they are not sent to us. Google says backups are end-to-end encrypted on Android 9 and newer when the phone has a screen lock. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings.

2. Suggestions from parents (optional)

The app's Parents area, protected by a grown-ups-only question, has a suggestion box. Using it is optional. When a parent sends a suggestion, the app sends:

  • the suggestion text (up to 1,000 characters)
  • a name, only if the parent types one (the name field is optional and empty by default; up to 60 characters)
  • the app version and the Android version (for example "Android 15")
  • the date and time it was sent

Where it goes. The suggestion is sent over a secure (HTTPS) connection to our database in Google Firebase Cloud Firestore, a service run by Google. The app does not use any other Firebase features and no analytics. The app itself cannot read back or change suggestions once they are sent.

When you are offline. A suggestion is kept on the phone until our database confirms it was received, and the app tries again later (when it is opened, or when another suggestion is sent).

Why we use it. Only to read your ideas and problem reports and improve the app. We do not use suggestions for advertising and we do not contact you through the app.

Please do not include personal details (such as a child's name, address or phone number) in a suggestion.

How long we keep it. We keep suggestions for up to 12 months and then delete them by hand.

Deleting a suggestion. Email hamid.arshad15@gmail.com. Because the app has no accounts, please tell us roughly when you sent it and what it said (or the name you entered), so we can find it. We will delete it.

Technical information Google may receive. Like any internet service, Google's servers receive the network (IP) address of the phone that sends a suggestion, as part of handling the request. Google processes this under its own terms and privacy policy.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app shows a full-screen ad after a finished quiz or game, never during play, and at most about once every three minutes. Ads are provided by Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK).

The app asks Google to treat every ad request as for a child and under the age of consent, to show only ads rated G (general audiences), and not to personalise ads. The app also does not request permission to use the Android advertising ID. Google states that with these child settings the advertising ID is not sent, and personalised ads and remarketing are turned off.

Even so, Google's SDK collects some information to show ads, measure them and prevent fraud. According to Google's published disclosure for its Mobile Ads SDK, this information may include:

  • the phone's IP address, which can be used to estimate a general location
  • interactions with ads and the app (for example app launch, taps, video views)
  • diagnostic information (for example app start time, hang rate, energy use)
  • device identifiers such as the "app set ID"

Google shares this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and encrypts it in transit. Google publishes this list for the current version of its SDK; the exact information can differ between SDK versions and is decided by Google, not by us.

When the app starts, Google's advertising component contacts Google to check whether a consent message is needed in your region. Like any internet request, this reveals the phone's IP address to Google. Google does not publish further details of this check.

Google's advertising component also stores some technical timestamps on the phone, for example when the app was first opened. The app does not include Google Analytics for Firebase. In our own testing, this component reported that its measurement upload was disabled; this is what we observed, not a guarantee for every phone or installation.

More about how Google uses information: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and Google's Privacy Policy https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Removing ads. A parent can remove all ads for good with a one-time purchase in the Parents area. After that the app no longer requests or shows ads. Google's advertising component is still started when the app opens, and may perform the start-up check described above.

4. Purchases (Google Play)

"Remove ads" and optional tips are paid only through Google Play. Google Play handles the payment and the payment details; we never receive card numbers or other payment information. The app only asks Google Play whether "Remove ads" has been bought on the Google account, so it can switch ads off, and remembers the answer on the phone. Purchases are covered by Google's Privacy Policy and Google Play's terms. Google Play's billing component may also send technical information to Google as part of providing purchases; this is handled by Google under its Privacy Policy.

Purchases and tips are only possible in the Parents area, behind the grown-ups-only question.

5. Speech

The app speaks letters, words and numbers. Many sounds are recordings built into the app; the Kalma, duas and Islamic words are only ever played from these recordings. Other words, mostly English, are spoken by the text-to-speech engine installed on the phone (for example Google's speech services): the app passes the word to that engine on the phone, and briefly starts the engine when the app opens so the first word plays quickly. The app itself does not send speech or text to us.

Whether the speech engine does its work on the phone or uses an online service depends on the phone, the speech engine and voice installed, and its settings. This is controlled by the engine's provider, not by the app, and is covered by that provider's privacy policy. The app does not use the microphone.

6. Permissions the app uses

  • Internet and network status: for ads, the suggestion box and Google Play purchases. All learning works offline.
  • Storage (only on Android 9 and older): to save "My World" pictures to the gallery. Newer Android versions need no permission for this.
  • Google Play billing: for "Remove ads" and tips.
  • Run in the background, start after reboot, keep the phone awake briefly: added by Android's WorkManager library, which Google's advertising component uses to schedule its background tasks. The app's own code does not use them.

The app does not use location, camera, microphone, contacts, or the advertising ID permission.

7. Sharing and selling

We do not sell information and do not share it with anyone except the Google services named above (Firebase, AdMob, Google Play), which process it to provide those services. Google may process information in other countries where it operates.

8. Children's privacy

The app is made for children aged about 2 to 6. We do not ask children for personal information and we do not knowingly collect it. The suggestion box, purchases, settings and the link to this policy are in the Parents area, which is protected by a grown-ups-only question.

If you believe your child has entered personal information in a suggestion, email us and we will delete it.

9. Security

Suggestions are sent over encrypted (HTTPS) connections. Our database's security rules only allow new suggestions to be added; they do not allow suggestions to be read, changed or deleted through the app. No method of sending or storing information is completely secure, but we keep what we collect to a minimum.
